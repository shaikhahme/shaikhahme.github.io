# Shaikh’s Virtues

**I read this page when I forget that the challenges I complain about today are the ones I fought for yesterday.**

_AI Disclosure: This was written entirely by me_

> “Don’t chase success, chase capability. Then success will chase you!”

A roughly translated quote from 3 Idiots, a movie that cemented my love and ambitions of engineering. Like many 
invisible directive forces, the movie affected me in ways I can only recognise now, 
more than 15 years later. These forces had been guiding my decisions for years, but I had never really 
made the effort to identify or formalise them.

It took a rejection, and a follow-up question from the Head of Cyber Threat Management at IBM, 
“What are your virtues?”  to make me stop and put words to them.

Virtues are inherently difficult to articulate because they are so deeply embedded in who we are. 
LUCKILY, I have enjoyed enough psychology to recognise the concept floating in my head: **eudaimonia**, 
one of the philosophical inspirations behind PERMA (a psychology framework).

I know now that what I strive for is **eudaimonia** ("Flourishing Life") And, for me, that means a career that feeds this.
I can split that pursuit into three major pillars.

## 1. Epistēmē: The need to learn why

> “If I could afford it, I’d study forever.”

I have said this long enough that I've forgotten where I heard it first.

A career in which I am intellectually challenged in new ways has always excited me. I still remember my first official cybersecurity lecture in Paris. I caught myself feeling nervous and excited, almost as if I were going on a first date. I still laugh at the feeling
and the incredibly nerdy thought that popped to my head - "truly a city of love".

There is something deeply rewarding about FINALLY understanding the intuition behind something, the moment that to me feels like I just unlocked 
a new lens to see the world.

In a perhaps optimistically, constructive and nihilistic way, understanding something gives it meaning and existence. 
I want to understand the world around me, and I feel that I can only fully experience it by trying to 
understand as much of it and in as many perspectives as I can.

## 2. Koinōnia: The need to help my community

> “I am a mosaic of everyone I’ve ever met.”

It is difficult not to feel a sense of responsibility towards, and be inspired by, your community when it feels like a part of you.

Specifically hard not to care for my “village” when my extended family alone is around 100 people, and the friends I have made 
across each of the seven cities I have lived, easily add another 110 (Yes, I maintain a list, so I don’t lose touch).

And that list obviously does not include every interesting, kind stranger or OSS dev (mini-shoutout) who has left some small piece of themselves with me.

When so much of who I am has been shaped by other people, contributing something back feels less like an obligation 
and more like a natural consequence of belonging to them.

I may be an engineer, but first, I am a villager.

## 3. Aretē: The need to excel

> “When the going gets tough, the tough get going.”

My dad used that line when I was crying about not getting into the football team I wanted as a kid. Sure enough, After a year of trying my best at every practice, there I was, playing CDM.

That experience taught me something I have enjoyed repeatedly since: there is an innate joy in working hard at something 
you love and becoming ~~good~~great at it.

Excellence, to me, is not simply about being better than someone else. It is the pursuit of capability, 
seeing how far I 
can develop my craft and how much can i maximise my positive impact.

My affinity for technology naturally led me towards a career where that pursuit is never ending.

## Where they intersect

This was the final piece of the puzzle. Looking back, cybersecurity simply appeared at the intersection of 
all three needs:

**A domain where I could always learn, help people, and excel.**

It seemed almost too perfect too! Today, that same need to help has pulled me towards what 
I believe to be one of the most exciting and potentially dangerous technologies since the Industrial 
Revolution, Artificial Intelligence.

That is why I began studying AI security, governance, and alignment in the hope of contributing, 
however I can, towards steering this incredible technology in a direction that benefits the community.

Now, when I feel drained after hours of coding, reading papers, cooking for my friends or simply escaping a rabbit hole 
triggered via Kurzgesagt. I tell myself

**Learn. Help. Excel.**

**Epistēmē. Koinōnia. Aretē.**

Disclosure: My feelings about Artifical Intellignece (or better yet LLMs - im refusing to give it the attribute of intelligence yet) are conflicting. While as an engineer it an incredible piece of technology, as a securtiy manager the widescale risk is evident. Therefore I 
truly believe that it is something that requires strong education, gaurdrails and most importanly allignment.
